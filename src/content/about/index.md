Dr Nurdiana Zainol Abidin is a Senior Lecturer at Pusat Kanser Tun Abdullah Ahmad Badawi (PKTAAB), Universiti Sains Malaysia (USM). Her research focuses on musculoskeletal health, body composition, physical function and healthy ageing, with particular interests in sarcopenia, osteosarcopenic obesity and women's health.

Her earlier research examined the relationships between muscle, bone, adiposity and vitamin D in postmenopausal women, alongside objectively measured physical activity among older adults. More recently, her work has expanded into computational approaches, including artificial intelligence and machine learning for developing and evaluating clinical screening models. Using large population datasets such as the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), she investigates approaches to identifying low muscle mass and low bone density, particularly in populations where conventional screening methods may be less effective.

Her research also explores emerging technologies for supporting physical and cognitive health. As principal investigator, she leads a Fundamental Research Grant Scheme (FRGS) project investigating resveratrol-loaded dissolving microneedle patches for exercise-induced muscle soreness, alongside a research project examining fully immersive virtual reality-based dual-task training in older adults with mild cognitive impairment. Her microneedle research received a Gold Medal and was First Runner-Up in the Special Category at the USM–AUSC International Sports Innovation Challenge 2025.

Beyond laboratory and clinical research, she is interested in translating scientific knowledge into accessible tools for researchers, practitioners and communities. This includes the development of PRIME-Active, a digital platform intended to support community-based physical activity programmes and research among older adults.

Dr Nurdiana is involved in postgraduate education, academic leadership and research supervision. She served as Head of Programme for the MSc in Clinical Exercise Science for the 2023/2024, 2024/2025 and 2025/2026 cohorts. Her teaching interests include applied and clinical exercise physiology, complemented by digital learning tools such as PhysioVerse 3D, an interactive biomechanics learning platform.

She completed her PhD in Biosciences at the University of Nottingham in 2021, investigating screening criteria for osteosarcopenic obesity and its association with bioavailable 25-hydroxyvitamin D. She previously obtained an MSc in Health Sciences from Universiti Sains Malaysia and a BSc in Biomedical Sciences from International Islamic University Malaysia (IIUM).

Her broader academic interests lie in integrating exercise science, biomedical research, computational methods and emerging technologies to improve health assessment, physical function and quality of life as people age.
